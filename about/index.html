About APARN

APARN is a network for artists, researchers, educators, curators and practitioners who work through art to make knowledge. It brings together people working in, from and with the Asia Pacific, whether they are based in a university, a collective or an independent practice.

Part of the Society for Artistic Research

APARN is part of the Society for Artistic Research: it is one of SAR’s Special Interest Groups. SAR is a non-profit, member-led international society for artistic research, established in 2010 in Bern.

Contributions from APARN events can be published in APARN’s channel of the Journal for Artistic Research (JAR), SAR’s open-access journal. Channel pieces are editorially reviewed, not peer reviewed. Papers can also be reworked and submitted to JAR’s peer-reviewed issues through its open calls.

Part of the SIG’s work is to address coloniality in artistic research, and to grow a broader, more inclusive network of practitioners from across the region.

What we are here to do

  1. 1Map artistic research initiatives and activities across the Asia Pacific.
  2. 2Make practical collaboration easier between people and institutions in the region.
  3. 3Understand the local cultural dynamics that shape artistic research here.

APARN is run by volunteers. Its convenors have each hosted a meeting from their own resources, and the network has no budget or separate administration.

How it began

  1. 2019APARN starts as a partnership between the Centre of Visual Art at the University of Melbourne and the Indonesian Institute of the Arts Yogyakarta (ISI Yogyakarta).
  2. October 2019The first meeting is held in Yogyakarta with the International Conference on Asia Pacific Arts Studies.
  3. 2020–2022Annual meetings continue online: Rebordering the Archipelago (2020), a graduate research meet-up (2021) and #APARN2022.
  4. 2022–2023APARN becomes a Special Interest Group of the Society for Artistic Research. Its inaugural SIG meeting closes #APARN2022, and aparn.net dates the SIG to 2023.
  5. 2023–2026The annual meeting travels: Yogyakarta, Cyberjaya, Bangkok and Singapore. APARN2025 is the largest so far, with over 150 presentations from 22 countries.
  6. 2026Dr Roopesh Sitharan (Multimedia University) becomes Director of APARN.

The first meeting, in October 2019, brought together Kurniawan Adi Saputro, Danny Butt, Alison Carroll, Pornrat Damrhung, Léuli Eshrāghi, Michael Hornblow, Khalid Midam and Helly Minarti, with a keynote by Gunalan Nadarajan.

Director and convenors

The convenors look after the network. Each conference also has its own organising team, listed in the archive.

Malaysia

Dr Roopesh Sitharan

Director of APARN (from 2026) / Network convenor

Faculty of Creative Multimedia, Multimedia University (MMU), Cyberjaya

A multidisciplinary practitioner, educator, curator, researcher and artist. His projects have appeared at the Gwangju Biennale, ISEA and SIGGRAPH, and his writing in Leonardo Electronic Almanac, CTRL+P and Documenta 12.

Work with APARN
  • Convenor of APARN2024, hosted at MMU
  • Convenor of APARN2025
  • Co-curator of Chapalang (Singapore, 2026)
  • The person to contact about proposals for APARN’s JAR channel

rs@mmu.edu.my

Indonesia

Dr Kurniawan Adi Saputro

Network convenor

Indonesian Institute of the Arts Yogyakarta (ISI Yogyakarta)

Lecturer in the Department of Photography at ISI Yogyakarta, teaching research methods in arts and visual communication. His recent research looks at photographs in climate education, community-based festivals and climate justice. In late 2024 he built Wana Nagara, a studio around a tiny urban forest for researching culture in a time of climate crisis.

Work with APARN
  • Co-led the network in its first years, with Danny Butt
  • Convenor of APARN2023, hosted at ISI Yogyakarta
  • Convenor of the 2020–2025 meetings
  • Co-editor of Rebordering the Archipelago
  • Co-editor of APARN’s JAR channel
  • Co-developed the Climate/Coloniality residency with PRAKSIS Oslo (2025)

kurniawan.as@isi.ac.id

Coordinator of Research for Design and Production at the Victorian College of the Arts, University of Melbourne. He wrote Artistic Research in the Future Academy (Intellect / University of Chicago Press, 2017) and sits on the Editorial Board of the Journal for Artistic Research.

Work with APARN
  • Co-led the network in its first years, with Kurniawan Adi Saputro

  • Co-editor of Rebordering the Archipelago
  • Co-editor of APARN’s JAR channel
  • Co-developed the Climate/Coloniality residency with PRAKSIS Oslo (2025)

butt@unimelb.edu.au

Thailand

Professor Pornrat Damrhung

Network convenor

Department of Dramatic Arts, Chulalongkorn University, Bangkok

Professor of Dramatic Arts at Chulalongkorn University and Associate Member of the Royal Society of Thailand. She specialises in contemporary dance cultures, community performance and young people’s theatre.

Work with APARN
  • Took part in the first meeting (2019)
  • Presented at #APARN2022 with Lowell Skar

Pornrat.D@chula.ac.th

Professor Gunalan Nadarajan

Network convenor

Penny W. Stamps School of Art and Design, University of Michigan

An art theorist and curator working where art, science and technology meet. His publications have been translated into 16 languages. His curatorial work includes Documenta XI (Kassel, 2002) and ISEA2008 in Singapore.
